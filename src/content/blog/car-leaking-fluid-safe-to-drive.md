An unidentified leak should be checked before you assume the car is safe to drive. **If you smell fuel, lose braking ability, see an oil-pressure warning, or the engine overheats, stop safely, switch off, and arrange assistance.** Keep away from suspected fuel leaks and ignition sources.

A small puddle can be harmless condensation, but size and color alone cannot establish that. AAA recommends identifying a leak and its severity before continuing to drive. [AAA’s maintenance guidance](https://www.aaa.com/autorepair/articles/time-stamped-car-maintenance-checklist).

## Is it water or a vehicle fluid?

Air-conditioning condensation can leave water underneath a parked car. Oil, coolant, transmission fluid, and other fluids can also reach the ground. Fluids vary by specification and change appearance with age, so “red means transmission” or “green means coolant” is not a reliable diagnosis.

Do not taste a fluid or touch it to identify it. Keep children and animals away. If safe, photograph the puddle from beside the vehicle and note where it sits relative to the front wheels or engine. Do not crawl under a vehicle supported only by a jack.

## Details that help identify the source

Tell the shop whether the leak appeared after a service, after using the air conditioning, while parked overnight, or only with the engine running. Mention warning lights, changes in shifting or steering, unusual smells noticed from a safe distance, and whether the puddle is growing.

A photograph helps start the conversation. It cannot reveal a leak hidden above an undertray or confirm that the brakes, lubrication, or cooling system are functioning safely.

## What happens at an inspection?

The technician identifies the fluid and traces it back to its source. Fluid can travel along panels before dripping, so the puddle’s location is a clue rather than proof. The repair recommendation should explain the leak, its urgency, and any related damage or low-fluid condition.

Ask whether further cleaning or testing is needed to separate an old spill from an active leak. Avoid adding a stop-leak product before diagnosis; check the vehicle manufacturer’s instructions before putting any additive into a system.
