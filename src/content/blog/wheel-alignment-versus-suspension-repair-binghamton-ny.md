## What an alignment is

Wheel alignment is geometry. Camber, caster, and toe are angles. The rack measures them and we adjust the ones that are adjustable on your car.

A correct alignment helps the car track straight, keeps the steering from fighting you, and stops the tires from wiping themselves out on one edge. It does not repair a leaking strut. It does not replace a torn control-arm bushing. It does not free a seized brake caliper that makes the car pull.

You need an appointment because the job needs the rack. Request a quote, then we put you on the schedule. Many alignment-only visits are same day.

## What suspension repair is

Suspension is the hardware that holds those angles in the real world: struts, shocks, springs, bushings, ball joints, links. Broome County potholes, freeze-thaw heaves, and railroad crossings around Binghamton beat on that hardware.

Worn suspension shows up as:

- A bounce that keeps going after a bump
- Clunks or rattles over broken pavement
- Nose dive when you brake, or a rear that squats when you accelerate
- A pull that comes back a week after an alignment
- Tires wearing on the inner or outer shoulder
- A steering wheel that is off-center even after someone "threw an alignment at it"

If the parts that set the geometry are loose or collapsed, the rack will show numbers you can force into spec for a day. Then the pothole on the way home undoes it.

## How to tell which one you are dealing with

You do not need the textbook name. Use what the car is doing.

**Mostly a pull, steering off-center, or feathered tire wear, and the ride still feels tight.** Alignment may be the main event. We still check tires, brakes, and the obvious worn joints first. A pulled car with a dragging brake will not be fixed on the rack.

**Mostly bounce, clunk, or a ride that feels like the tires are disconnected from the body.** Start with suspension inspection. Align after the parts that hold the alignment are healthy.

**Both.** That is common. A leaking strut and a car that sits a little low will wear tires and pull. Replacing the strut without aligning is how you get a straight-ish car with a steering wheel that sits crooked. Aligning without replacing the strut is how you are back in two months.

**Uneven tire wear alone.** Look at the pattern. Inner-edge wear on both fronts often points to toe or camber issues, which may be adjustable, or to worn inner bushings that let the wheel tuck. One tire only can be inflation, a bent wheel, or a single bad corner. Photograph the tread. We will tell you what we can see before you drive over from Binghamton.

## What a photo can tell us, and what it cannot

Tire photos show a wear pattern. A video shows bounce. An alignment sheet from another shop shows what they measured that day.

A photo cannot confirm a ball joint under load or a leaking strut until we see the car. Alignments happen on the rack, in person. That is why we schedule them instead of promising a driveway adjustment.
