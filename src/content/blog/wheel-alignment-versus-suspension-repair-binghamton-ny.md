A car can pull even when an alignment has been performed because alignment is only one possible cause. Unequal tire pressure, tire condition, brake drag, and worn steering or suspension components can also affect how it tracks. [Firestone explains causes of pulling after alignment](https://www.firestonecompleteautocare.com/blog/alignment/what-to-do-when-car-pulls-to-one-side/).

If the pull is sudden, severe, or accompanied by difficulty steering or braking, stop safely and arrange assistance. Do not try to correct a dangerous pull by driving faster or letting go of the wheel.

## Start with the symptom and the paperwork

Tell the shop whether the pull stayed the same after the alignment or appeared afterward. Does it happen all the time, under acceleration, or when braking? Is the steering wheel off-center even when the car follows a straight path? Those are different observations and should be recorded separately.

Bring the before-and-after alignment report and any tire or suspension invoice. Ask what was adjusted, whether readings met the vehicle’s specifications, and whether anything could not be brought into range.

## Why a green alignment report may not settle it

The report describes measured angles at the time of the work. It does not by itself establish tire condition, rule out a dragging brake, or show how worn parts behave on the road. A useful follow-up checks the vehicle as a system.

A technician can decide whether controlled tire-position testing is appropriate for the tire type, size, and rotation restrictions. Do not swap tires at random or reverse directional tires to test an internet theory.

## What if the tires already wore unevenly?

Correcting wheel angles does not put lost rubber back on a tire. Existing wear can remain relevant to handling after the alignment. The tires need their own condition assessment, along with the cause of the wear. Read [why tires wear unevenly or on the inside](/blog/uneven-inside-tire-wear/).

## Should I pay for another alignment?

Contact the original shop first and explain that the symptom remains. Ask what its follow-up policy covers and what additional checks it recommends. Repeating the same adjustment without finding the cause can mean paying again without resolving the complaint.
