**Avoid continuing to drive a car that is actively misfiring.** If the check-engine light flashes or the engine shakes severely, move to a safe stopping place, switch the engine off, and arrange roadside assistance. Continuing to drive can cause further damage.

A misfire means combustion did not happen as expected in one or more cylinders. It can feel like a stumble, uneven idle, hesitation, or loss of power. A technician needs to distinguish it from vibration caused by tires, mounts, or other components.

## Why a flashing light matters

Unburned fuel from a misfire can overheat and damage the catalytic converter. A flashing warning therefore deserves a different response from simply booking a routine maintenance visit. Your owner’s manual gives the instructions for your vehicle. [Ford’s owner-manual guidance on a flashing malfunction indicator](https://www.fordservicecontent.com/Ford_Content/Catalog/owner_information/Falcon_FGX_Sedan_OM_Print5_February2015_revised.pdf) explains the risk of converter damage.

Even if the shaking stops, report when it happened and have it checked. An intermittent fault can return under load or at a particular temperature.

## Does a misfire mean I need spark plugs?

Not necessarily. Ignition, fuel delivery, air leaks, and mechanical problems can produce similar symptoms. Spark plugs or an ignition coil may be involved, but a parts list based only on the symptom leaves too much unanswered.

Useful questions for the technician include: Which cylinder or cylinders are affected? Under what conditions does it occur? What test supports the recommended repair? If several parts are recommended, which have failed and which are maintenance items?

## What information helps the shop?

Once safely parked, write down whether it happened on a cold start, at idle, while accelerating, or after the engine warmed up. Include the year, make, model, mileage, any codes already read, and recent repairs or fuel stops. There is no need to recreate the problem on the road or film while driving.
