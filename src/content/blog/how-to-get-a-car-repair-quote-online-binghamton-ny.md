## What an online quote actually is here

A lot of "get a quote online" pages are price lists, chatbots, or national lead forms that bounce you to whoever bought the zip code.

That is not this shop.

We do not publish repair prices on the website. The number you get is an estimate based on the photos and details you send. It can change after a technician inspects the car in person. No repair starts without your approval.

Labor is quoted against the Mitchell 1 Labor Structure, an industry labor guide, so the time on the estimate is grounded in a standard, not a guess from the driveway.

If that sounds slower than a magic online price, it is also how you avoid a surprise on the bill.

## How the online form actually works

Open [Request Service](/quote) on your phone. The first screen asks which sounds like you.

**I know what needs repair.** You already know it is front pads, a battery, or a hose. Tell us what needs to be done. Example: "Front brake pads and rotors need replacing."

**I'm not sure what's wrong.** Describe what the car is doing. Example: "Grinding when I brake, and the check engine light came on this week." You do not have to name the system.

**I already have an estimate.** You have paperwork from a dealer or another shop and you want a second look. Example: "The dealer quoted struts and an alignment. Is that fair?" Photograph the estimate. We will look at the line items with you.

Next you add your name, phone, year/make/model if you have it, and any photos or short video. Mileage and VIN help when you have them. They are not required.

Then we call or text you back, usually within the hour during shop hours, with a real number.

## What to photograph (this is the part most people skip)

Half the battle with car trouble is explaining it over the phone. A photo puts us on the same driveway as you.

Send whatever you have:

- A cracked hose, wet spot, or rusty fitting
- The dashboard with the warning light on
- The puddle on the pavement, plus a close shot of the color
- Pads, rotors, or a wheel if you can see them safely
- Another shop's printed or emailed estimate

You do not need a perfect photo. You do need the subject in the frame, in focus, with enough light to see color. If a clip is dark or cropped, we will ask for another. That is faster than guessing.

A short video helps when the problem is a noise: grinding on the brakes, a clunk over a bump, a slow crank. Film the dash or the wheel area while it happens, if you can do that safely.

## What a photo can tell us, and what it cannot

From a photo we can often tell you what we are looking at, whether it looks like a same-day job, and what to expect before you drive over from Binghamton.

From a photo we cannot promise the final bill. Hidden rust, seized hardware, a second leak, or a code that points somewhere else only shows up on the lift.

A few honest limits we already tell customers:

- Intermittent problems sometimes need a road test or a return visit. We will say so instead of pretending one photo caught it.
- Alignments need the rack. Request the quote, then we get you on the schedule.
- Diagnostic fees vary by vehicle and concern. If you send a photo or a code, we can often tell you what to expect before you bring the car in.
- If the job is outside our lane, we say so up front. We will not take a deposit and figure it out later.

That last point matters. A quote you can trust is also a shop that will tell you no.

## What this looks like in practice

These are the patterns we see from Binghamton and the rest of the Tri-Cities. They are typical, not named case files.

**The puddle on Court Street, before work.** Someone texts a photo of green or orange fluid on the pavement. Color and location often tell us coolant versus washer fluid versus oil. We tell them whether it is reasonable to drive to Airport Rd or whether they should wait. Same-day cooling-system work is common when parts are on hand.

**The grind that started after a week of rain.** A photo of the wheel or a short video of the noise is enough to treat it as brakes until we see the car. We inspect pads, rotors, calipers, hardware, and fluid, and we show you what is worn when we can. If only the fronts are done, we will not push a four-corner job without a reason.

**The dealer packet.** Someone uploads an estimate for struts, an alignment, and a list of "while we are in there" items. We read the line items against Mitchell 1 labor times and tell them what looks necessary, what can wait, and whether we can do the work. A second look is one of the three reasons the form exists.

**The check-engine light on 17.** A photo of the dash plus the year, make, and model is a start. The useful quote usually needs a scan in the bay: we pull the codes and explain what they mean before anything gets touched. No scare tactics, no replace-everything list.

None of those start with you knowing the part name. They start with you showing us the car.
