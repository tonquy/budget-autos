Uneven tire wear means the tread is not wearing consistently across the tire or around its circumference. Inside-edge wear can be easy to miss from beside the car, so the tread needs checking across its full width.

**If you see exposed cords, a bulge, separation, or severe damage, do not drive on that tire.** Arrange professional help. Bridgestone recommends evaluation of irregular wear and tire damage; its [tire maintenance and safety manual](https://www.bridgestonetire.com/content/dam/consumer/bst/na/warranties/bs_maintenance_and_safety_us_en_2024v2.pdf) also explains the role of correct inflation and maintenance.

## Does inside wear mean the alignment is wrong?

Wheel angles are one possibility. Suspension condition, changes in ride height, and tire pressures may also be relevant. A worn inner edge is a reason to inspect and measure, not proof that a specific part has failed.

Ask for tread-depth readings across the tire and an explanation of the alignment measurements. A photograph of the outside shoulder alone can miss the worst wear.

## What do different wear patterns tell you?

Wear concentrated on one shoulder, a feathered feel across tread blocks, and repeated dips around the tire suggest different inspection paths. They are clues for a technician to combine with pressure checks, wheel condition, suspension inspection, and service history.

Use the vehicle’s tire-information placard and owner’s manual for pressure specifications. The maximum pressure printed on the tire sidewall is not the vehicle’s everyday inflation recommendation.

## Do I need tires, an alignment, or suspension work?

Sometimes more than one is needed. Replacing a damaged tire addresses its condition; diagnosing why it wore helps protect the replacement. An alignment cannot restore missing tread, and new tires cannot repair a loose or worn suspension component.

Ask the shop to separate the work into what makes the current tires unsafe, what caused the wear, and what maintenance will help prevent recurrence. Follow the vehicle and tire manufacturer’s rules for compatible replacements, especially where tire-size or tread-depth differences matter to the drivetrain.
