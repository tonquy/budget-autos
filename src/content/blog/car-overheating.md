**Pull over safely and switch off an overheating engine. Do not keep driving to reach the shop.** Arrange roadside assistance and follow the vehicle’s owner’s manual. Never remove a radiator or coolant-reservoir cap while the system is hot; pressurized coolant can cause serious burns. [Ford’s coolant safety guidance](https://www.ford.com.au/support/how-tos/owner-resources/vehicle-maintenance/how-do-i-check-engine-coolant-levels).

## What can cause overheating?

Possible causes include coolant loss, a thermostat that is not operating correctly, a cooling-fan problem, restricted circulation, or a water-pump fault. More than one problem may be involved. A temperature warning alone cannot identify a head-gasket failure or tell you which part to buy.

The pattern helps the technician: did it start in traffic, at road speed, after a coolant repair, or during a long climb? Report what you already observed; do not run the engine again to reproduce overheating.

## It cooled down. Can I drive again?

A falling gauge after the engine is switched off does not show that the cause has gone away. A leak or circulation problem can cause the temperature to rise again soon after restarting. Get advice based on the vehicle and symptoms, and arrange transport when overheating is unresolved.

Topping up a reservoir does not repair a leak. Use only the coolant and procedure specified for your vehicle, and do not open the cooling system while hot. If there is steam, stay clear rather than leaning over the engine bay.

## What should an overheating inspection explain?

Ask the shop to identify the evidence behind the diagnosis: where coolant is escaping, whether the fans operate when required, and whether the system holds pressure and circulates properly. The exact tests depend on the vehicle. If internal engine damage is suspected, ask what findings support that conclusion before deciding on a major repair.

Write down warning messages, any visible puddle, recent cooling-system work, and how long the engine ran after the warning appeared. Those details are more useful than guessing from a replacement part you found online.
