A check engine light on a Binghamton morning does not tell you the job. It tells you the car stored a fault. The light on Route 17, at a light in downtown Binghamton, or in a Vestal parking lot is the same lamp. The cause is not.

## What the light is trying to say

The lamp is a messenger. It is not a diagnosis.

**A solid check engine light** usually means the computer saw a fault that is still present or recently present. You can often drive the car, but you should not ignore it for weeks.

**A flashing check engine light** is different. That often means an active misfire that can overheat the catalytic converter. If it is flashing, reduce load, avoid highway speeds if you can do so safely, and [call (607) 323-0236](tel:+16073230236). Do not wait on a form.

Other lights get mixed in. Oil pressure, temperature, battery, and brake lamps are not "check engine." Photograph the dash so we can see which symbol you actually have.

## Causes we see all the time

These are common, not a complete catalog. Your car may not match the internet's favorite guess.

**Gas cap and EVAP leaks.** A loose, cracked, or missing cap after filling up in Binghamton is a classic. The evaporative system is picky. The light can take a few drive cycles to clear even after you click the cap. A smoke test in the bay finds the leaks a cap swap will not fix.

**Oxygen sensors.** They measure exhaust and help the computer mix fuel. A lazy sensor can hurt mileage and trip the light without stranding you on Court Street. Replacing sensors because a code "sounds like O2" without testing is how people buy parts twice.

**Misfires: plugs, coils, injectors, or air leaks.** A rough idle, a stumble off the line, or a flash of the lamp under load often lives here. Short trips in winter, oil-fouled plugs, and moisture in coil wells after a thaw all show up in this climate.

**Mass air flow or intake leaks.** Unmetered air after the sensor, a dirty MAF, or a torn boot will confuse the mix. You may notice hesitation more than a dramatic breakdown.

**Catalytic converter efficiency codes.** Sometimes the converter is actually tired. Sometimes upstream misfires or a failing sensor set it up to fail. Throwing a converter at a car that is still misfiring wastes money. We explain that before parts get ordered.

**Thermostat, coolant temp, or running rich or lean.** A car that never reaches temperature on a two-mile hop from Binghamton to Johnson City can store mixture codes. So can a thermostat stuck open or closed. Overheating is a "call us" problem, not a "clear the code" problem.

**Wiring and connectors.** Salt, potholes, and engine heat crack insulation and loosen plugs. Electrical faults rarely fix themselves. We [test instead of throwing parts at a maybe](/services/electrical-systems).

A parts-store printout with a code number is a starting point. It is not a repair order. We scan, look at live data, and explain what it means for this vehicle before we quote the fix.
